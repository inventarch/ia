Evaluator catalog extension: the producer side of trusted evaluator catalog and obligations is implemented by C25–C29 below (EVAL-01..04, OBL-01..03 including the strict-typing-v1 profile mapping; qualification cases EVAL-Q01, Q02, Q07 and Q08, and the producer side of Q03, in tests/evaluator-catalog.test.ts). Still open and owned elsewhere: the API transactional controller and managed transition seam (spec §4, LK-06), the ia-apps fixed-to-catalog consumer (LK-05), the consumer-side cases EVAL-Q04–Q06 (late edit/reevaluation, concurrent workers and durable intent, silent agents), the consumer side of EVAL-Q03 (a real compiler evaluator and excluded-source detection). Status: implemented through stage C and the D/R/E integration clause C21; the projection clauses C22–C24 are retired (below). Task evidence below is chronological; statements of later work within early checkpoints describe that checkpoint only. Shared owners: systems design sections 3–5 and language section 4.3.

## Instance schemas

C01. Pure `validateSchema(record, registry, pool)` returns a `COMP-SCHEMA` assessment with outcome pass, fail or not-evaluated and namespaced findings. `pool` holds the admitted occurrences that can supply a counted endpoint (C06), for an `out` or an `in` rule alike: every record the subject's own assertions name, and every admitted record that may assert the relation toward the subject in the reciprocal spelling; the subject itself is always considered. Callers pass the whole admitted set. A record named by the subject's own assertion but absent from the pool leaves an obligation it could change not-evaluated (EDGE-UNRESOLVED); a reciprocal author absent from the pool contributes nothing and counts as absence. No I/O or parsing occurs here. Callers exclude language-error previews before validation. A separate verdict constructor adds the caller's explicit revision to assessments; no clock-derived revision. Only compliance emits verdicts.

C02. Resolve the subject's schema through its registered discriminator. A missing registration/schema or mismatched kind is one failure for that subject and consumes its subordinate checks. The floor's system/schema registrations use their actual native schema declarations when present; absence is not waived by this checker.

C03. Require each `must have` section. Under closed, diagnose every unlisted section. Repeated sections form one logical field collection. Cognition and activation are floor-owned and always admitted even under closed; their validity remains language-owned. Head is a virtual field section, never a required body section. Closed applies to keys as well as section names (C04b); open admits any key.

C04. For each schema field, use its section and exact key. The current dialect admits exactly section.key with a single-token key; nested paths and schema spelling of multiword keys are not added here. Must fields require a present field. An absent optional section creates no subordinate field obligation. A missing required section consumes missing-field reports beneath it. Validate every occurrence, so one valid duplicate does not mask a wrong-typed duplicate. A declared key whose type is not `list of T` is exactly one field: `must have` means exactly one for non-list types and `may have` at most one. A second occurrence of such a key, counted across repeated sections and in the virtual head section, is FIELD-DUPLICATE, reported once per schema row at the second occurrence's span, while every occurrence is still type-checked and narrowed (C04a). Only a `list of T` key may repeat. Undeclared keys are not counted (D3, work-system design O-6, 2026-09-25); under open they remain admitted, under closed they are FIELD-UNKNOWN (C04b). Missing required field: FIELD-MISSING; bad present value: FIELD-TYPE. One failed value, including a bad list member, yields one finding at the field.

C04b. Under `closed`, a declared body section admits only the keys the schema declares for it (D1 / W0-L4, work-system design O-6, 2026-09-25). Every other ordinary field is FIELD-UNKNOWN (error) at its own span, `closed @schema <name> does not declare '<section>.<key>'`; the children of an undeclared block fold into its one finding, `- item` lines are not keys, and W0 narrowing still applies to the declared keys beside it. An ordinary field is a compiled field the compiler left as a field. Exempt, because the language reads them itself: the head (virtual, open); the floor grammar sections `relationships`, `cognition` and `activation` (edges, cells, selectors); a field the compiler consumed as a semantic product, decided by span, namely a governance variant clause (`record.variants`; `readVariants` takes every `governance` field except `severity` and `when`) or a contract requirement clause (`record.requirements`, the `REQ-*` lines); and a `section.key` path listed in the kernel `DIMENSION_PATHS` (today `governance.severity`), which the graph reads as a coordinate. Records of the two floor dialect words `schema` and `system` are exempt whole: their body is the schema/system dialect (`sections.must have`, `fields.may have`, `edges.may`, `closed`), and `@schema schema` is closed with no declared field. The two dialect words and the three grammar sections are the only name-based exemptions; every other carrier is recognised by what the compiler emitted for it.

C04a. A field whose schema row carries a W0 narrowing (language L19a) is checked further once its value matches its type, each narrowing yielding at most one finding per field occurrence, list items included: `values` (from `id in [...]`) requires every scalar or quoted text to be a member, compared exactly, else FIELD-VALUE; `target` (from `ref to <word>`) requires every reference's discriminator to equal the word, else FIELD-REF-TARGET, and otherwise, when the caller supplied a reference lookup, warns FIELD-REF-MISSING for a reference no admitted record declares; `form` (from `text form <form>`) requires every text to match the form, else FIELD-FORM. `validateSchema` looks references up in its pool (the subject included) through `resolveTarget` over the candidates sharing the reference's word and name; `validateGraphSchema` looks them up in the admitted graph. An ambiguous target is not missing: identity ties are graph-owned. A narrowed field with no matching value, an absent optional section, or a value that already failed its type raises no narrowing finding.

C05. Value semantics (`matchesType`) and text forms (`matchesForm`, C04a): `iso-date` admits exactly `YYYY-MM-DD` where the month is 01–12 and the day exists in that month, leap years by the Gregorian rule; no time, zone or single-digit part. Text admits scalar, string or prose (including empty quoted text); id admits scalar/string matching an ASCII letter followed by letters/digits/hyphens; qname admits scalar/string with exactly four nonempty canonical identity slots (lowercase system/kind/facet/name, closed kind); number admits a finite decimal scalar/string with optional sign, fraction and exponent, no empty/hex/Infinity; ref admits only typed sigil references; flag admits none (a bare key) or scalar true/false. Block is not a flag. `list of T` requires a list and validates every item against T; an empty list is valid because the dialect has no nonempty-list constraint. Type recognition is over compiled values, not lexical matching of IA source.

C06. Edge obligations use logical active predicates in the direction the rule's spelling carries: an `out` rule counts the record's active edges by their target endpoint, an `in` rule the active edges whose target is the record by their source endpoint. Either relation may be asserted on the record or, in the reciprocal spelling, on another admitted record. Count distinct (endpoint identity, fragment) pairs, where a fragment joins the pair only when it addresses the counted endpoint: an assertion on the record contributes its fragment, and a reciprocal assertion's fragment addresses the record and is dropped. Equivalent reciprocal assertions do not inflate cardinality. Match the counted endpoint by closed kind or discriminator. Required one/one-or-more means at least one; one and optional cap at one even for a may row. A required field with type flag is present when its bare key occurs. A must optional row has zero minimum (optional explicitly permits absence). A may one-or-more row permits absence but has no maximum. Unconditional edges can establish a static obligation. Conditioned edges or unresolved/ambiguous targets that could change its answer produce EDGE-UNRESOLVED and not-evaluated unless an independently certain failure already establishes fail. A dangling edge cannot be counted as a pass. This checker does not select a coordinate or execute contracts.

C07. Findings use language's location/severity/message shape with IA-COMP codes; stable order is path, line, code, message. Codes: SCHEMA-MISSING, SCHEMA-KIND-MISMATCH, SECTION-MISSING, SECTION-UNKNOWN, FIELD-MISSING, FIELD-UNKNOWN, FIELD-DUPLICATE, FIELD-TYPE, FIELD-VALUE, FIELD-REF-TARGET, FIELD-REF-MISSING, FIELD-FORM, EDGE-CARDINALITY, EDGE-UNRESOLVED. All are errors except EDGE-UNRESOLVED (warning with not-evaluated) and FIELD-REF-MISSING (warning; the assessment still passes, so admission is not blocked by a dangling typed reference). A failed check dominates not-evaluated. Source spans refer to the offending instance; missing structure refers to the containing record or section/block.

## Native system admission (Plan 4 Task 3)

C08. `validateSystems(folders, registry, pool)` is pure. A folder supplies its canonical relative path, name, parsed sources and admitted compiled records. The host supplies all discovered folders, including empty ones. Each must contain exactly one system declaration, located at its direct system.ia with the same name. A malformed folder receives SYSTEM-MALFORMED once and consumes its subordinate ownership/steward checks. Parser/registry faults remain owned by language; the host excludes their previews before this check.

C09. Every admitted record in a valid folder uses the folder's own registered word, a word from a directly required system, or a fixed floor word. Floor system/schema are always visible and need no extra requires item. Unregistered words remain language-owned. A foreign registered word yields DISCRIMINATOR-FOREIGN at that record. Nested records are independent subjects. Full resolved dependency order must place each declared requirement before its dependent; a missing registration/system remains language-owned. BOOTSTRAP-ORDER diagnoses a supplied inconsistent order, not a separate hardcoded sorting policy.

C10. A steward reference must be @agent, resolve to exactly one admitted agent occurrence inside that folder, and have an unconditioned governance.applies whose value is a list of discriminator ids including every word minted there. Missing/ambiguous/wrong-kind/foreign steward or missing applies coverage yields one STEWARD-MISSING per system naming missing coverage. This list is an ownership declaration, not execution permission. Agent-system self-steward is admitted; other folders require agent-system directly. The kernel is not a system folder and has its separate exemption.

C11. Each active registration's word must occur on either side of at least one consent row, with wildcard matching; absence yields CONSENT-EMPTY warning and does not invalidate structural admission. Each visible schema is enrolled by exactly one active registration, counting fixed floor registrations. Zero yields SCHEMA-UNREFERENCED; more than one yields SCHEMA-MULTIPLE. Independent warning evidence can coexist with pass; only an unavailable evaluation is not-evaluated. Assessments returned are COMP-SYSTEM, COMP-STEWARD, COMP-BOOTSTRAP, COMP-SCHEMA-ENROLLED and COMP-CONSENT-DECLARED. Actual edge consent remains a separate check.

C12. Native systems own README/SPEC next to their records. The first five and their record closure must compile and pass instance/admission checks without substitute TypeScript providers. Their stewards and methods are authored roles/procedures; this task does not launch agents, execute scenarios or install a hook. Success/refusal mutation tests must use the real floor/system sources. Native sources are authored, not generated from legacy catalogs.

Task 3 evidence: native system admission is exported and consumed by pnpm native:check. The five-system closure has 153 records and 170 passing structural assessments. Nineteen native mutation/connection tests cover C08-C12, alongside 62 schema tests and 21 kernel tests; the language suite remains 1001 passing tests. Typecheck/build pass. No scenario engine or host execution is claimed.

## Full graph and revision checks (stage C)

C13. The full suite consumes an immutable Graph and explicit source diagnostics/folder descriptions. Its registry is graph.registry, and graph winners alone supply semantic subjects. A report retains verdicts keyed by check/scope with graph.revision and a stable aggregate outcome (fail dominates not-evaluated, which dominates pass). Language and graph findings retain their original codes, spans and severity; no second wrapper finding repeats the same fault. Findings sort by path, line, code and message. Exposed results are immutable snapshots. Source diagnostics are supplied explicitly, so absence of source evaluation evidence is not a parse pass.

C14. Full graph schema validation shares C02-C05 field rules but counts admitted graph active edges under C06 in the rule's direction: an `out` rule reads the record's outgoing active edges and their `to` endpoint, an `in` rule the active edges whose target is the record and their `from` endpoint. Late-resolved edges count, rejected-consent edges do not. A graph-owned edge refusal leaves the dependent predicate obligation not-evaluated without a second absence diagnostic. Cardinality keys follow C06, using the edge's fragmentEndpoint to tell which endpoint a fragment addresses. Unresolved/conditioned edges retain uncertainty; do not count a condition as unconditional satisfaction. The early validateSchema(record,registry,pool) API remains for bootstrap without a graph.

C15. COMP-FRAGMENT checks resolved references on every graph edge: phase/Primitive must name an existing target cell and a requirement fragment must name an existing target requirement. Use fragmentEndpoint after inverse normalization. A missing target remains graph-owned; no duplicate missing-fragment error is added. Invalid resolved fragment: FRAGMENT-MISSING. Coverage accepts unconditional active implement edges from a case to a contract requirement, using active direction; the fragment must address the contract endpoint rather than an inverse assertion's case endpoint. Every contract requirement needs a declared case; each contract needs a success case and a failure or refusal case. Conditional/dangling edges cannot establish that static coverage. COVERAGE-MISSING names uncovered requirements; COVERAGE-KIND names missing case classes when requirement coverage itself is complete; uncovered requirements consume subordinate case-class reports. Coverage does not execute cases or satisfy adoption.

C16. COMP-CONSENT and COMP-IDENTITY retain graph-owned refusal findings and report unresolved relationships as not-evaluated where consent cannot be determined. Shadowing is retained as report evidence, not a tie error. COMP-SELECTOR checks compiled terms against closed language axes/values; invalid caller-supplied products cannot establish a pass. COMP-PARSE consumes supplied language diagnostics; a missing set of observations is not-evaluated. COMP-CHECK validates each check's evaluator id against the implemented inventory, reading `check.implementation` when present and `check.runs` otherwise (`checkRunner`; obligation occurrences carry the id it resolves, and the composition compiler applies the same precedence); malformed/missing/unknown ids produce CHECK-UNKNOWN once per declaration, and a check naming both fields with different values produces CHECK-CONFLICT. Check declarations do not turn a check on or supply execution evidence.

C17. COMP-VARIANT performs exact finite satisfiability per record/key family. Search equal-specificity pairs for overlap not covered by higher-specificity clauses, with absent routing axes included and dimensions read as graph G08. One witness per ambiguous family produces VARIANT-AMBIGUOUS at the first conflicting clause and names the competing source lines and canonical request coordinate. No arbitrary domain-product bound or cutoff; prune impossible/dominated regions. A lower tie covered at every coordinate by stronger clauses is valid. An unconditional family can tie at a coordinate with omitted axes even when every fully supplied coordinate has a stronger clause.

C18. COMP-ADOPTION concerns each admitted active require relationship from an adopter to an @contract. Resolve its requirement clauses using their conditions only when an explicit coordinate is supplied; otherwise a conditional clause remains not-evaluated. Evaluators are supplied explicitly by contract identity and requirement id, receive the graph/adopter/contract/requirement and return pass/fail/not-evaluated plus explanatory text. Missing evaluators and thrown evaluator errors are not-evaluated with named evidence; a reported violation is ADOPTION-FAILED. This is structural verification only, not a code interpreter or permission to execute capability effects. No requirements is not proof of an authored contract; language owns contract shape.

C18 repository consumer: [tools/compliance](../../tools/compliance/SPEC.md#foundation-authoring-adoption) supplies the three foundation-authoring callbacks from actually executed native source scenarios. That adapter checks source revision, adopter and view before using observations, retains missing/stale evidence as not-evaluated, and reports observed violations as failures. This is explicit host registration, not a package default. Database/runtime reports without callbacks remain not-evaluated, and declaration-only COMP-COVERAGE never satisfies COMP-ADOPTION.

C19. COMP-KERNEL and COMP-FIXTURES require caller-supplied matching-revision assessments from actual verification. No supplied result or a stale result produces NOT-EVALUATED. A pure language-fixture runner can consume source and expected products/diagnostics/format companions without reading files; tools discover fixtures and record observed results. Every named refusal must have a tested failing case; unavailable fixture coverage stays explicit. General prose scenario execution remains outside this stage.

C20. NOT-EVALUATED is warning evidence that changes the outcome; warning consent-empty/shadow evidence does not fail an otherwise completed check. ## Trusted evaluator catalog, obligations and evidence (spec-0007 producer)

C25 (catalog v1, EVAL-01). Pure `createEvaluatorCatalog({format, entries}, {codecs, builtins?})`. The top level must be exactly `{format, entries}` with format `ia-evaluator-catalog/1.<minor>`; an unknown key (e.g. `revocations`), another major or another shape is CATALOG-VERSION. Minor-version handling: any minor of major 1 is accepted, but every entry must use exactly the v1 fields, so a later minor that adds behavior is refused rather than silently ignored; the declared format, minor included, is part of the digest. An entry has exactly id, contractVersion, implementationVersion, implementationDigest, inputSchemaId, outputSchemaId, supportedScopes, effects, environmentProfile, limits, evidencePolicy and availability; an unknown or missing field is CATALOG-ENTRY-INVALID. The id is a lowercase namespaced `id` (at least one hyphen, e.g. `acme-strict-typing`) so it stays a valid native `check.runs` value. Only CHECK_IDS are built in (D4). `options.builtins` lists host-reserved ids (validated as `A-Z`/`a-z` ids, otherwise TypeError); an entry id equal case-insensitively to a CHECK_IDS or reserved id, or in the reserved `comp` namespace, is CATALOG-BUILTIN-OVERRIDE. Reserved ids are recorded as `catalog.reserved`; they never pass COMP-CHECK and never resolve as evaluators. Versions are MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH; a contractVersion major outside EVALUATOR_CONTRACT_MAJORS (1) is CATALOG-VERSION. implementationDigest is `sha256:<64 hex>`. inputSchemaId/outputSchemaId are `name@major` ids that must be in the host codec registry, otherwise CATALOG-CODEC-UNKNOWN; a codec is trusted host code with `validate` and an optional `normalize`. supportedScopes (nonempty) and effects are unique ids. environmentProfile is `{id, credentials: none | scoped}`; ambient credentials are not expressible. limits require positive safe-integer timeoutMs, memoryBytes, outputBytes and concurrency (CATALOG-LIMITS). evidencePolicy is `{kind: fresh-run}` or `{kind: reusable, maxAgeMs, environmentSensitive}`. availability is `{status: supported}` or `{status: unavailable | revoked, reason}` with a printable single-line reason of at most 200 characters and no credential assignment. A repeated id@implementationVersion is CATALOG-DUPLICATE when the digests agree and CATALOG-DIGEST-CONFLICT when they differ; more than one supported version of one id is CATALOG-DUPLICATE. Findings carry the 1-based input index of the offending entry. Any finding refuses the whole catalog with no partial result. The accepted catalog is deeply frozen, entries sorted by id and version, with digest `sha256` of the canonical serialization of the format, entries, built-in and reserved ids; equal inputs in any entry order give equal digests. Only objects returned by this constructor are accepted by the functions below (`isEvaluatorCatalog`); a copied or hand-built object is a TypeError.

C26 (check.runs with a catalog, EVAL-02, EVAL-Q01/Q02). `validateCheck(node, catalog?)` and `evaluate(graph, {catalog})`. Without a catalog the result is byte-identical to C16. With one, a CHECK_IDS id passes as before; otherwise `selectEvaluator` picks the id's supported entry, else its highest listed version. Supported passes. Revoked is EVALUATOR-REVOKED, a blocking error. Unavailable is an EVALUATOR-UNAVAILABLE warning and the assessment is not-evaluated (D5): an unavailable evaluator does not refute the declaration, and blocking belongs to admission of a required obligation, while an optional one stays visibly unevaluated (EVAL-02). An id in neither set, including a host-reserved id, is still CHECK-UNKNOWN. Native check records keep `check.runs`; the schema also admits `check.implementation`, which `checkRunner` reads first, and requires neither alone (C16). Catalog text never names a callback, command or path: `bindEvaluators(catalog, implementations)` associates trusted host implementations keyed `${id}@${implementationDigest}`, and any key naming an unknown, substituted, unavailable or revoked implementation is EVALUATOR-SUBSTITUTED and refuses the whole binding. The host must bind before invoking; binding invokes nothing, and admission independently checks each receipt's evaluator id, version and digest against the catalog (C28).

C27 (obligation resolution, OBL-01..03, EVAL-02). Pure `resolveObligations(input)` takes the subject, policy revision, catalog, the catalog digest the policy pins, captured scope facts (JSON data), the root adoption set, the selected law/contract/check occurrences (identity, word, adoption, requirement fragment, runs, provenance `{path, line}`, optional applicability predicate id, required flag and JSON input), trusted versioned applicability predicates (`name@version`) and package metadata directives. It returns a deeply frozen resolution sorted by `obligationId` (`obl:` sha256 of subject, occurrence, requirement and runs), each with subject, requirement, provenance (path, line, adoption, source), applicability, required, evaluator identity (built-ins use version `builtin`, digest `builtin:<id>` and fresh-run evidence), `inputDigest` and evidence policy. The input must be JSON data and must satisfy the evaluator's input codec; the digest covers the codec-normalized value, so equivalent inputs share a digest. Predicates receive a deeply frozen copy of the facts, never the caller's object. Applicability is applicable, not-applicable or undetermined. not-applicable always carries resolver evidence: `basis: predicate` with the predicate id@version, or `basis: package-metadata` with the directive source. A missing, throwing or malformed predicate is undetermined with a fixed message that never echoes predicate output. Undetermined required applicability is OBLIGATION-UNDETERMINED (blocking). OBLIGATION-UNRESOLVED covers: a malformed occurrence (missing provenance included) or adoption; an adoption or runs id that does not resolve (a reserved id never resolves, D4); an input that is not JSON or fails its codec; a package directive that is malformed (the action set is closed to `optional` and `disable`, and the source must be sanitized) or names no selected obligation (M3). OBLIGATION-CONFLICT covers a pinned catalog digest that differs, one adoption declared with conflicting source or mandatory status, and one obligation selected twice with different content. Either refuses with status `refused` and no obligations. A root-adopted mandatory obligation is always required. Package metadata never changes any root-adopted obligation, whether the root made it required, optional or mandatory: the directive is ignored with an OBLIGATION-WEAKENED warning (D1; spec-0007 OBL-01, source precedence cannot silently weaken root requirements). Metadata may relax or disable package-adopted obligations only. Every resolution carries `digest = resolutionDigest(content)` over its canonical content, which includes the catalog digest and policy revision, and is recorded as issued by this module (D2). `inspectObligations(input)` returns the same selection as rows with support (builtin, supported, unavailable, revoked or unknown), versions, applicability and required flag, including unresolved rows, and never calls an evaluator. Predicates and codecs are the only host code called.

C28 (receipts and admission, EVAL-03/04, EVAL-Q07/Q08). Receipt format `ia-evidence-receipt/1` has exactly format, receiptId, attemptId, obligationId, subject, inputDigest, digests {candidate, native, code, policy, dependency}, environment {id, toolchain}, evaluator {id, implementationVersion, implementationDigest}, catalogDigest, policyRevision, observedAt, completedAt, result (passed, failed, unavailable, error, cancelled, not-evaluated, not-applicable), evidenceDigest, issuer and trust (local-unsigned or service-associated). Text fields, receiptId and issuer included, must be sanitized single-line text. `validateReceipt(receipt, expectation, now)` takes an explicit ISO-8601 UTC `now`; it and every receipt time must be a real calendar instant (round-trip checked, so 2026-02-30 is invalid). The checks run in this order:
- shape and time order: RECEIPT-INVALID
- obligation and subject: RECEIPT-SUBJECT
- evaluator known to the catalog with the same digest: RECEIPT-EVALUATOR
- evaluator not revoked: RECEIPT-REVOKED
- evaluator equal to the obligation's evaluator: RECEIPT-EVALUATOR
- code digest: RECEIPT-CODE
- toolchain: RECEIPT-TOOLCHAIN
- trust: RECEIPT-UNTRUSTED (D3). Trust is never self-asserted. A service-associated receipt counts only when the host passed an explicit `verifyServiceReceipt` callback that returns true for a frozen copy of it; the issuer string alone never elevates trust. `minimumTrust` is required, with no default: `service-associated` refuses local receipts.
- candidate, native, policy, dependency and input digests, policy revision, catalog digest and, for fresh-run or environment-sensitive policy, environment id: RECEIPT-STALE
- fresh-run attempt identity: RECEIPT-FRESH-RUN
- reusable maximum age: RECEIPT-EXPIRED

`evidenceKey` is the EVAL-04 deduplication key over candidate, obligation, evaluator, policy revision and digest, the environment `{id, toolchain}` (toolchain only when environment-insensitive; no other fields) and, for fresh-run, the attempt.

`acceptEvaluatorOutput(catalog, obligation, observation, {secrets?})` never throws for evaluator output. It maps a host observation to a result:
- unsupported evaluators are `unavailable`;
- timeouts, crashes, a missing or oversized byte length, output that is not JSON data (a Date, Map, function, cycle or non-finite number), an envelope other than `{result: passed | failed, evidence, diagnostics?}`, a hostile object that throws on read, and codec rejection or throw are all `error`, with EVALUATOR-OUTPUT-INVALID and fixed messages.

Failed diagnostics are sliced to 20 entries before filtering, and each line is redacted and then truncated to 240 characters. Redaction runs in this order: control characters are normalized to spaces (in the lines and in the supplied secrets, M2), then supplied secret values, bearer tokens, quoted keys (`"password":"x"`), `KEY=value` or `KEY: value` where the key contains token, secret, password, passwd, api_key, credential, auth or private_key as a substring (e.g. `GITHUB_TOKEN=`, `AWS_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY=`), and `key 'value'` forms are replaced by `[redacted]`. A built-in obligation is a TypeError there. `adaptBuiltinVerdict(obligation, verdict?)` maps a built-in verdict to the envelope: pass is passed, fail is failed, not-evaluated or absent is not-evaluated (spec §5 preserves unevaluated built-in outcomes).

`admitTransition(resolution, receipts, context, now)` first verifies the resolution (D2): its digest must recompute from its content, and it must either be resolver-issued in this process (the frozen, branded object) or match `context.expectedResolutionDigest` pinned by the host. Every obligation must be structurally sound: the id recomputes; built-in evaluators are CHECK_IDS with the built-in digest; catalog evaluators match the catalog's id, version, digest, contract version and evidence policy; not-applicable carries predicate or package-metadata evidence and never package-metadata on a root adoption. Otherwise the decision is OBLIGATION-UNVERIFIED and not admitted. The digest is an integrity check, not authentication: a resolution that crosses a trust boundary must have its digest pinned by the host. Admission then requires the same catalog and policy, and that every required applicable obligation has a valid passed receipt of at least `minimumTrust` as its latest valid result (a same-time non-pass wins). The following block a required obligation:
- failed (OBLIGATION-FAILED);
- unavailable, error, cancelled, not-evaluated, evaluator-claimed not-applicable, or missing or invalid evidence (OBLIGATION-BLOCKED);
- undetermined applicability;
- an unavailable or revoked evaluator.

Resolver not-applicable with evidence does not block. An optional obligation never blocks and is reported as `optional-unevaluated`, or `optional-failed` when its latest valid receipt failed. Each decision records the trust of the receipt it relied on, and the transition records `minimumTrust` and `reliedTrust` (the weakest trust among satisfying receipts, or none). A local unsigned receipt is a local diagnostic. None of these functions reads files, a clock or the network, schedules or executes anything, or persists receipts.

C29 (evaluation profiles, OBL-02; facilitator decision D6). `EVALUATION_PROFILES['strict-typing-v1']` is the consumer contract id mapping to three requirements: `configuration`, `included-file-coverage` and `compiler-run`. `profileOccurrences('strict-typing-v1', binding)` expands one selected law or contract occurrence into three required occurrences with requirements `strict-typing-v1/<name>`, each discharged by a host catalog evaluator named in the binding. A built-in id, a non-catalog id, an unknown profile or a missing or extra mapping is a TypeError. The profile is not a native word. Declarations, native cases and check records never become results by presence: the three obligations are admitted only with valid passed receipts, so a real compiler run requires an installed evaluator and its receipt.
