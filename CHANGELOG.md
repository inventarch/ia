### positionBody adds the applies-by-word section, playbook cells and mandates, and reserves blocking rules by word

@inventarch/runtime's positionBody, and so position, adds four sections to the ia.position-body.v1 body, whose format name is unchanged: appliesByWord, appliesByWordTallies, cells and mandates, after pointerTallies; counts gains appliesByWord and truncatedAppliesByWord. The AppliesByWord, PositionCell and SubjectMatch types are exported from the package root and @inventarch/runtime/internal/position, and PositionVia gains a subject variant, {by: 'subject', label: 'field match, not a row', matches}. appliesByWord lists the `@law`, `@convention`, `@principle` and `@playbook` records of the seat's closure whose unconditionally stated subject.subject-word is the word, or subject.subject-kind the kind, of a loaded record, the seat included, or of a blocking candidate reserved in rules, which the body delivers in full as it does a loaded record; nothing applies by word to a rule that rules holds by word alone. That is a field match: it follows no row and claims no relation. Each record is listed once, at hop 0, with every field that matches and the first loaded or reserved record answering it, and with its re-seat key {seat, shape, phase, depth: 1, budget: 16}, ranked by band descending then identity; the first 48 are listed and the rest tallied per word and owner system. An entry may also be loaded or a pointer. rules now also holds every blocking `@law`, `@convention` or `@principle` other than the seat that applies by word to a candidate, whichever candidates the budget truncates or the word leaves out, so a blocking rule is delivered at budget 0 and under any word whether it applies by claim, by row or by word, and no entry of rules is in appliesByWord, pointers or the frontier. cells gives, for the first four playbooks in appliesByWord, the cell at the key's phase and the shape's primitive as {playbook, address, text}, address being <identity>#<phase>/<Primitive> and text what ia read reads at that address; a playbook without one gives {playbook, missing, nearest?, message}, nearest being the closest phase with a cell of that primitive in the cycle orient, plan, act, learn, a tie going to the phase that order lists first. Besides deciding a conditional relation row, which seeding-engine follows only where it holds, the phase changes only the cells and the keys spelled with it. mandates lists the `@mandate` records of the closure that claim a location seat or whose authority.scope names the seat's workspace, each once and at hop 0, a mandate that does both keeping its claim, by band descending then identity. A word set on the key filters none of the four sections: they list rules, playbooks and mandates of every word, an applies-by-word match naming the first answering record of what loads under the word; and no record at runtime placement enters them. Limits: a cell's condition is not evaluated, so every cell at the address is delivered; no mandate is matched by its participant; and an `@agent` record's governance.applies is not read.

Packages: `@inventarch/runtime`.

### position returns body(K) with its digest and host note, and runtime-band records stay out of the body

@inventarch/runtime exports position(handle, within, partial?) from the package root and @inventarch/runtime/internal/position, with the PositionOutput, HostNote and Freshness types. It completes the partial key with normalizeScopeKey, resolves it with resolveScopeKey through the scope token within, assembles the body with positionBody and returns the frozen {body, digest, hostNote}. digest is the @inventarch/graph codec digest of the body. hostNote is undigested host state, {revision, capturedRevision?, previousRevision?, freshness, key}: revision is the handle's revision; capturedRevision is the revision of the capture ia capture last wrote and previousRevision that of the previous snapshot the handle retains, each omitted when there is none; freshness is current when the capture is at revision, stale when it is at another and no-capture when there is none; and key is the key normalizeScopeKey completed, a location seat as the caller spelled it. The key's parts stay closed, so a text, token or within part is refused as an unknown part (IA-RUNTIME-REQUEST-INVALID) and nothing text-driven runs. One key over two copies of a workspace at different roots gives byte-equal bodies and equal digests, and neither a context call with any text nor a written capture changes them. A position needs no capture: freshness is reported, never refused. positionBody now leaves records at runtime placement (band 0) out of the body: none is composition, a seed, a hop, loaded, a rule, a pointer, a tally or frontier, no row or claim of one ranks or reaches another record, none is the record a location is declared at, so a location whose declarer and claimants are all at runtime placement names the seat unknown, and a seat at runtime placement, K0's included, is refused with IA-RUNTIME-REQUEST-INVALID; a key whose seat the token does not admit (one resolved through another token) is refused with IA-RUNTIME-REQUEST-INVALID too. Limits: the body's revision and the handle's revision still hash every source, runtime-placed ones included, so a runtime-band source appearing moves the revision, and with it the digest and, against a capture taken before, the freshness; admission's findings still resolve references against every placement, so a runtime-band record that an authored loaded record's edge or field reference names (an observation a task's work.exit-evidence names, say) takes that reference's missing-target or missing-field-reference finding out of the body's unknowns, and the body then names that relation neither as a pointer nor as an unknown; a runtime-band `@workspace` or `@system` can still move the database's seat and root decisions the closure is built from, a location's resolved seat then naming no unknown; installed state and the CLI and adapter identities are not reported; and the nine version 1 routes and ia scope are unchanged.

Packages: `@inventarch/runtime`.

### ia capture --preview reports what a capture would write without writing; @inventarch/db exports planCapture

ia capture --preview [--json] admits the workspace and refuses exactly as ia capture does (a floor or seed input that fails to parse, a root whose .ia/src declares no `@workspace`, and the IA-DB-PATH-UNSAFE snapshot path checks, whose repair names ia capture --preview again), then reports what the capture would write and writes nothing: it creates no .ia/work directory, and .ia/work/snapshot/current.json and previous.json keep their bytes and modification times, so the retained previous snapshot is not rotated away. The human report says it is a preview and that nothing has been written, prints the capture's counts (changed, unchanged, new and removed against the prior capture), the snapshot's digest and size and the rotation the capture would make, lists every changed, new and removed identity in full under Changed, New and Removed headings, each sorted and printed with any terminal control character as visible escape text (a removed identity is read from the current.json on disk; --json carries each as it is), and ends by naming ia capture with --root as given, or ia validate when admission has errors. It exits as the capture would: 1 when admission has errors, else 0. With --json it prints the capture's result with preview: true after version and identities {changed, new, removed} after rotated; snapshot, digest, previous and rotated describe what the capture would write. A system error a preview meets reading the snapshot pair is not reported as a path to make writable. ia capture without --preview writes and reports exactly as before, with no new key, and ia capture --help lists --preview. @inventarch/db exports planCapture(root, text) and the CapturePlan type from its root: the checks and reads writeCapture(root, text) makes before its first write, with the same TypeError and IA-DB-PATH-UNSAFE refusals, returning the result writeCapture would return plus identities {changed, added, removed}, the identities its counts count, each sorted by code unit; it creates no directory and writes nothing. writeCapture plans through the same code, and its result and writes are unchanged. Limits: a preview reads the sources and the snapshot pair as they are when it runs, so a capture after a later edit reports what it then finds; workspace revisions, the frozen Door routes, MACHINE_PROTOCOL and ia scope are unchanged.

Packages: `@inventarch/cli`, `@inventarch/db`.

### Harden body reads and terminal delivery reports

The runtime body reader looks up source-locator fields only among declared table entries, so an admitted custom word named constructor reads its own body instead of failing on an inherited JavaScript property. Markdown body reads ignore headings inside list and quote containers, keep fences within their containers and reject invalid backtick fence openers, preserving top-level section boundaries and exact source bytes, and read a document in time linear in its length, up to a logarithmic factor in how deeply its containers nest: each ATX heading and its inline links in time linear in the line, each list or quote marker a line opens or continues in time linear in the marker, and a blank line inside nested list items without walking them, so a long run of spaces, unclosed brackets or nested list markers, or blank lines inside deeply nested items, before an anchor no longer stalls a read, on the Door's read and the MCP ia_read included. The human ia next and ia position reports escape terminal control characters in source-derived text before layout and styling, and ia read escapes them in its header while printing the body as read; JSON and Door values remain unchanged. Clean installed-package qualification, and the tools suite against the built CLI, now exercise capture idempotence, read bodies and digests, next dependency ordering and self-declared status, read-only effects, refusal paths and the retained 1.x compile artifact contract.

Packages: `@inventarch/cli`, `@inventarch/runtime`.

### The Door serves read and position inside its scope, as MACHINE_PROTOCOL version 2

@inventarch/runtime: the Door accepts two new operations, read and position. read takes params {within?, locator, includeRuntime?} and answers {ok: true, result} with the ReadBody readBody returns ({locator, identity, kind, path?, digest, body, certified: false}) or that function's read refusal. It always reads through a scope token, the initial boundary when within is omitted. Through a token narrower than the whole workspace, one bound to a root below the workspace root, a phase or identities, or issued under such a token (the scope operation issues one when it is given any of them, and a door opened with a boundary that sets one starts with one), a locator that no admitted record in the scope answers, whether its record is outside the scope, refused by admission or absent, is one plain IA-RUNTIME-READ-UNADMITTED, 'The locator is not in this scope', which names no path, line, identity or refused record. Through a whole-workspace token, one that @inventarch/db isCompleteScope proves complete (the initial token of a door opened without a boundary, as the MCP door's is, or with one that sets none of them, and a token the scope operation issues from such a token without setting any), a miss is refused as readBody refuses it without a token: a record admission refused is named with its path, line and reason, an identity no source holds is not in this workspace's sources, and a line says what the workspace's sources hold in its file. The door reads documents through the new DoorOptions.read, by default @inventarch/db readWorkspaceBytes over its root bounded at 4 MiB, and names a document it cannot read by its workspace-relative path, never by the server's root: a root that can no longer be opened, as when the workspace moves while the door serves, is refused IA-RUNTIME-READ-UNREACHABLE, as every failure of the reader is, its message carrying the default reader's IA-DB-ROOT-INVALID, 'The workspace can no longer be opened to read <path>', which names the document alone. It binds adopted mounts once, when it opens, from .ia/workspace.json, unless it was opened with explicit adopted captures, which name no directory. ReadBodyOptions gains an optional within: given a scope token, readBody reads get and records in that scope and refuses every miss in a narrower scope with that plain refusal, and every miss in a whole-workspace one as it refuses it without a token. ReadRefusal gains an optional reason, admission's reason, on the refusal of an identity whose record admission refused, read without within or through a whole-workspace token; without within readBody is otherwise unchanged. position takes params {within?, seat?, shape?, phase?, depth?, budget?, word?} and answers {ok: true, result} with the {body, digest, hostNote} that position returns for that partial key through the door's scope token, byte for byte: no params is K0, and the other parts take normalizeScopeKey's defaults. Request text, a token or any other parameter is refused as an unknown parameter, so nothing text-driven runs. A refusal of the key is {ok: false, code: IA-RUNTIME-REQUEST-INVALID, message, next}, next naming one command: ia position --shape <context|governance|execution|sequence|learning> or ia position --phase <orient|plan|act|learn> for a part outside its closed set, ia position --depth 2 or ia position --budget 64 for a part outside its cap, ia vocabulary for a word that is no string or that the closure does not register, ia position for a seat of another form, a seat the scope does not admit or a path outside the workspace, and for a seat at runtime placement ia position, or ia inspect when that seat is the one K0 takes, the repository's own `@workspace`, whether the key names it or not. RuntimeError gains an optional next, which normalizeScopeKey and resolveScopeKey now set on each key refusal they throw, so position throws it too; the code and message of every refusal are unchanged. The new DoorOptions.protocol is the MACHINE_PROTOCOL version a door serves, by default the current one: a door at version 1 refuses read and position as the unknown operation 1.1.0 refused, byte for byte, and a value that is not an integer from 1 to the current version is a TypeError. A version 1 operation's refusal, and every refusal the Door raises itself, keep 1.1.0's {ok, code, message}. MACHINE_PROTOCOL is version 2: ProtocolOperation gains an optional since, absent on the nine version 1 rows, whose JSON is byte-identical to 1.1.0's; the read row is appended with since: 2 and mcp: ia_read, and the position row, after next, with since: 2 and mcp: ia_position, each with one flow line. Code that expects MACHINE_PROTOCOL.version to be 1, reads every row as a CLI machine route, or pins the version 2 rows or their parameters should filter the rows by since or take the new rows. A version 2 door's unknown-operation refusal lists read, next and position, in that order, after the version 1 operations it admits. @inventarch/db exports readWorkspaceBytes(root, path, limit), which returns one workspace file's exact bytes and refuses a path that escapes the workspace, is not portable by the rule the distribution workspace file reader applies (NFC, at most 1024 code units, no backslash, control character or any of <>:"|?*, no segment that ends in a dot or a space or names a reserved device) or passes a link or junction, and a hard-linked or nonregular file (IA-DB-PATH-UNSAFE), a missing or unreadable file or one of more than limit bytes (IA-DB-SOURCE-UNAVAILABLE), and a file replaced or resized during the read (IA-DB-SOURCE-CHANGED); each refusal names the file by its canonical path alone. @inventarch/mcp-door serves ia_read and ia_position, whose inputSchemas are the read and position rows' params, so with ia_next it serves twelve tools. @inventarch/cli: runCli, the machine route that @inventarch/cli/internal/main exports and the host launcher's door mode calls, opens its door at protocol version 1, so read and position there are refused with exactly 1.1.0's bytes; in @inventarch/cli/internal/operation-help, describeOperation describes only the version 1 rows, and renderOperationSchema and renderOperationHelp label a version 1 row's description version 1, as 1.1.0 did, whatever the table's version, and a later row, should a caller hand them one, the version its since names. Limits: the CLI's machine routes stay the nine version 1 operations, so ia scope in every form, the other routes, their help and their --schema output are unchanged, and ia read stays the consumer verb, which returns the Door's body and digest and refuses a document the Door refuses with the same code, though its own reader names a link by its absolute local path; ia position, the consumer verb of the same output, runs the ia position commands its refusals name.

Packages: `@inventarch/cli`, `@inventarch/db`, `@inventarch/mcp-door`, `@inventarch/runtime`.

### The runtime renders the position packet for the claude and codex hosts, with a receipt

@inventarch/runtime exports renderHost(host, packet, digest, hostNote, target) and PACKET_MARKER from the package root and the new @inventarch/runtime/internal/packet-host subpath, with the PacketHost ('claude' | 'codex'), PacketTarget ('repository' | 'consumer'), HostFile, PacketReceipt and HostOutput types. renderHost renders one position packet, with the digest and host note renderPacket returned, as the host's files and returns the frozen HostOutput {files, receipt}; it reads no workspace and writes, deletes or plans no file. No host gets an agent file. claude renders .claude/rules/ia-workspace.md and .claude/skills/ia-authoring/SKILL.md for a consumer workspace, and CLAUDE.md, .claude/skills/ia-authoring/SKILL.md and .agents/skills/ia-authoring/SKILL.md for the repository target; codex renders AGENTS.md and .agents/skills/ia-authoring/SKILL.md for either. Every file carries the marker `<!-- Generated by ia project; edit the cited native records. -->`, first in the entry file and after each SKILL.md's YAML frontmatter, and each ia-authoring skill keeps the authoring guidance previously rendered by compliance for that target, its marker line aside. The entry file holds the packet's sections in the packet's field order (seat, participants, mandates, system pointer lines, captured-record tallies, intent table, phase table, command catalog, SPEC lines), plain Markdown with one entry per list item or table row, so it renders entryCount(packet) entries, byte-identical for both hosts; then the line `ia-generated <revision> <packet digest> <adapter>`, where the adapter is ia-claude or ia-codex and names the package version (`ia-claude@<version>`) only in the consumer target, so a committed repository file does not change when a release bumps the version; then, in the consumer target only, a 'Host note (not part of the packet)' section with the freshness, installed state and key. The receipt, format ia.packet-receipt.v1, holds host, adapter (id@version in either target), revision, packetDigest, bodyDigest, entries, files (each rendered file's path and the SHA-256 of its UTF-8 text), removed, foreign, guard, hostNote, participants and mandates (the packet's `@agent` and `@mandate` identities), slug, cli and writtenAt. An unknown host or target, or a digest that is not the packet's, is refused with IA-RUNTIME-REQUEST-INVALID. The CLI's ia host, ia project, initialization with a host and installation refresh use this adapter to write consumer files and receipts; the repository projection generator writes the repository files. These replace the retired compliance renderers described in the replace-renderers note. Limits: renderHost itself leaves removed and foreign empty, guard 'none', cli null and writtenAt null for its applying caller to set; CLI-written receipts carry the CLI's id@version. Receipts carry no source digests or per-command consumer versions, and the host note's installed state stays unknown.

Packages: `@inventarch/runtime`.

### Preserve full file identities in host and steward checks

Receipt preflight and the steward guard's legacy-agent and source-settings readers retain full-width file and directory identities. Distinct Windows file IDs that exceed JavaScript's safe integer range now refuse replacement instead of comparing equal after rounding. The source-settings reader validates the opened read-only handle before reading, refuses linked or replaced files and bounds reads through that same handle. Handle closure is preserved, and timestamp comparisons retain nanosecond precision.

Packages: `@inventarch/cli`, `@inventarch/steward-hook`.

### deliveryView reads one plan's delivery view through a scope token and writes nothing; the Door serves it as next, the MCP door as ia_next and the CLI as ia next

@inventarch/runtime exports deliveryView(handle, within, seat?) and NEXT_CODES from the package root and the new @inventarch/runtime/internal/next subpath, with the CycleRow, DeliveryMilestone, DeliveryResult, DeliveryTask, DeliveryView, ExitEvidence, NextCode, NextRefusal, Prerequisite, ReviewItem, StateDimension and StateLine types. deliveryView reads through the scope token within and writes nothing; only a whole-workspace token (db isCompleteScope) also reads the handle's refused records and the report's COMP-PARSE findings. The plan is the one a `@plan`, `@milestone` or `@task` seat belongs to or, without a seat, the only admitted `@plan` at band 100. It returns {ok: true, view}, the view being {format: ia.delivery-view.v1, revision, plan, milestones, tasks, review, next, summary}, or one refusal {ok: false, code, message, next, plans?, cycle?} whose next names one command: IA-RUNTIME-NEXT-SEAT (a seat the scope does not admit names the view without a seat that runs: ia next while the scope admits one `@plan` at band 100, ia next --seat <the first> while it admits several, ia next --help while it admits none; a record that is no plan, milestone or task names ia position --seat <seat>), IA-RUNTIME-NEXT-NO-PLAN (names ia next --help), IA-RUNTIME-NEXT-AMBIGUOUS (several plans at band 100, listed in plans; names ia next --seat <the first>) and IA-RUNTIME-NEXT-CYCLE (require rows that form a cycle among tasks, milestones or both, listed in cycle as declared rows; names ia position --seat <its first task, else its first milestone> --shape sequence). Tasks come in one prerequisites-first order (Kahn's algorithm, ties by milestone then task): a task follows the tasks and milestones it requires and those its milestone requires. Each task carries a basis line per effective prerequisite (a required `@decision` has a choice or not; a required `@task` has exit evidence or not; a required `@milestone` has exit evidence of its own, or for every one of its tasks, or not; a requirement no admitted record answers blocks, and so, as unknown, does one the task or its milestone declares whose answer the scope does not read), inherited lines marked (via <milestone>); one verdict line, exit evidence recorded (<observation>, evaluator <evaluator>[, self-attributed | , self-attribution unknown]), no declared blocker or blocked (<basis>; ...); its work.status, printed as self-declared and never a basis; and five state lines: intent, definition, realizable, realized and worked. Exit evidence is an `@observation` with verdict success whose evidence.subject names the record, or which the record's work.exit-evidence names, and whose evidence.subject-revision reads current under the db handle's readiness; ExitEvidence.attribution is self when evidence.evaluator equals the identity the task's work.owner-agent names, unknown when the scope answers no owner-agent the task states or the observation records no evaluator for a task that names one, else other. A relation row that carries a condition, such as requires or grounds with when phase is act, counts only where the condition holds at the coordinate the scope token binds (its phase, or none), decided as graph traversal decides it: the record that states the row decides its provenance and artifact-set terms, and a severity term only where its own severity decides it at every coordinate (graph effectiveSeverity takes the stronger of the record's severity and the coordinate's), while a term on an axis the scope binds no value for, the phase on the whole-workspace scope ia next reads and the severity on every scope, leaves the row undecided. A row whose condition fails is no row; an undecided row is conditional with an unknown basis and never satisfied: a conditional requirement orders nothing and blocks with the basis <word> <target>: conditional on <terms>, which the scope binds no <axes> to decide (unknown), and an intent an undecided grounding or supersession could change is unknown, while one that reads the same at every coordinate the scope could still bind, as when a decision that only may ground the task has no choice while another grounds it with no condition and is accepted, reads as it does everywhere (decision conditional-relations-in-delivery). No milestone is satisfied through its tasks, and the summary does not say that every task has exit evidence, while a task may go unread: on a narrowed scope, or on a whole-workspace scope while admission refuses a `@task` or a whole file. Review items name two or more live decisions grounding one record of the plan (one that another of them supersedes is not live, unless the two supersede each other in a cycle, which the item names), two accepted specs one of which supersedes the other with no replaced-scope, and, on a whole-workspace scope, each `@plan`, `@milestone` or `@task` admission refused and each file a language error refuses whole. next is ia position --seat <task> --shape sequence for the first task in order with no declared blocker, so tasks with exit evidence are skipped; it is null when every task has exit evidence or every other task is blocked, and summary says which. Limits: intent reads accepted or superseded only from a live grounding `@decision` that states a choice and an effective-revision; an intent an undecided row could change is read again at no more than 256 coordinates the scope could still bind, past which it stays unknown; realizable and realized read not applicable for a task; worked reads stale only against the previous retained snapshot, and unknown rather than unobserved on a narrowed scope; a refused `@task` is never a listed task, as its milestone cannot be read, and only a whole-workspace scope names it as a review item; a requirement declared only on a record outside the scope is not read; and other refused records, such as a `@decision` or an `@observation`, are read as absent and not reported. The Door serves the view as a new MACHINE_PROTOCOL version 2 operation, next, with params {within?, seat?}: it answers {ok: true, result} with the DeliveryView deliveryView returns through the door's scope token, the initial boundary when within is omitted, or with that function's refusal unchanged, next, plans and cycle included. A version 2 operation's refusal may so carry more than {ok, code, message}, while a version 1 operation's refusal, and every refusal the Door raises itself (an unknown or mistyped parameter, a token it did not issue, an unknown operation), keep 1.1.0's shape. A door serving protocol version 1, as the CLI's machine routes do, refuses next as the unknown operation 1.1.0 refused, byte for byte, and a version 2 door's unknown-operation refusal lists read, then next, after the version 1 operations it admits. MACHINE_PROTOCOL stays version 2 and appends the next row, with since: 2 and mcp: ia_next, after read, plus one flow line; the nine version 1 rows are unchanged, and code that pins the version 2 rows or their parameters must take the new row. On a whole-workspace token the view's admission review items name the `@plan`, `@milestone` and `@task` records admission refused, with path, line and reason, so ia_next discloses them on the MCP door's whole-workspace initial scope. @inventarch/mcp-door serves an eleventh tool, ia_next, whose inputSchema is the next row's params. @inventarch/cli adds the consumer verb ia next [--seat <plan|milestone|task>] [--json]. It prints the plan and its summary, its milestones, its tasks in order, each with its verdict line, its status marked self-declared, one requires line per prerequisite and its five state lines, then the review items and the command for the next task with no declared blocker, with --root as given; with --json it prints one line {version: 1, ok: true, view}, view being the Door's result. It opens the workspace without the db cache and writes nothing. Its refusals keep the runtime's codes and messages at exit 1, and each names one command with --root as given: for a seat the workspace does not admit, ia validate when admission refused that record, else ia next --seat <the nearest admitted plan, milestone or task> when one is near, else ia next when the workspace authors one plan at band 100, ia next --seat <the first> when it authors several, and with none what a missing plan names; ia position --seat <seat> for a record that is no plan, milestone or task, as the runtime names it; for a missing plan, ia validate when admission refused the plan a source holds, or with a seat the milestone or plan on the seat's way to its plan, else the records to author, then ia next again; ia next --seat <the first plan listed> for several; and for a cycle ia position --seat <record> --shape sequence at the record the runtime names, the cycle's first task by milestone then task, else the first milestone a row requires. ia next is not a machine route: LEGACY_OPERATIONS, the usage line, ia <operation> --help and --schema and every ia scope form are unchanged. The view's next is printed as the view gives it, with --root as given. RUNTIME_CODES gains the four IA-RUNTIME-NEXT codes, so code that switches exhaustively over RuntimeCode must handle them. @inventarch/runtime/internal/render adds statedText(record, field), which the body reader now shares; readBody is unchanged.

Packages: `@inventarch/cli`, `@inventarch/mcp-door`, `@inventarch/runtime`.

### ia position prints the position body for a scope key; the key's word no longer filters the reserved rules

@inventarch/cli adds the consumer verb ia position [--seat <id|path>] [--shape <H>] [--phase <P>] [--depth <d>] [--budget <n>] [--word <w>] [--json], which prints the runtime's position for the scope key its flags name, read through the whole-workspace scope: no flag is K0, the repository's `@workspace` at shape context, phase orient, depth 0 and budget 0, and a key naming any part takes normalizeScopeKey's defaults for the rest. A --seat that spells a canonical identity is that record; any other is a location, workspace-relative or absolute inside the root, and ./ makes a path spelled as an identity a location. Human output prints the seat and the body's revision, the key used, the capture's freshness (current, stale or no-capture, stale naming ia capture with --root as given), the body's digest and counts, then the loaded records, the pointers and their tallies, the rules and playbooks that apply by word with their tallies, the playbook cells, the rules reserved outside the budget, the mandates, the frontier and the unknowns, and last the two widening keys as ia position commands with --root as given: one hop deeper (absent at depth 2) and the re-seat template --seat <identity> at depth 1 and budget 16. ### Location seat resolution and the claimant index

@inventarch/graph indexes path selections once per load. Graph gains claims, one Claim {from, field, selection, source} for each text value a record states in authority.covers, subject.covers, work.covers or hook.paths (the list is exported as CLAIM_FIELDS; a `@check` claims no path, since its schema requires check.scope as prose), and referencesFrom, the typed field references keyed by holder; directedView reads referencesFrom instead of scanning every reference, and its output is unchanged. selects(selection, path) implements one path-selection dialect: workspace-relative segments separated by /, ** as a whole segment for any number of segments (none included), * for any run of characters within one segment, ? for one character, a trailing / for the directory and everything below it, and an exact path otherwise; matching is case-sensitive, a backslash reads as /, and there are no braces, classes, escapes or negation. An empty, absolute or non-canonical selection, and any path that is absolute or escapes the workspace, select nothing. isSelection(selection) says whether the dialect reads a selection at all. claimants(graph, path, scope) returns the records whose selections select a path as Claimant {identity, band, matches}, band descending then identity ascending, with every matching field and selection. The @inventarch/db readers (Handle, ReadHandle and the editor reader) add resolveSeat(path, options), which returns a SeatResolution {path, seat, by, claimants, unknown}: the seat is the admitted `@system` of the system folder holding the path (the floor's for .ia/src/floor), else the `@workspace` declaring the longest sources root that contains it, else, for a path in no .ia tree, the repository's own `@workspace` (the one in .ia/src that declares a root, else the only one there). With no seat, unknown reads undeclared, or outside for a path that is absolute or escapes the workspace. Options bind as get binds them, and a scope token prunes the seat and the claimants. The readers also add inertDeclarations(options), which names, with its record, field, value, path, line and reason, each declaration that declares nothing: a composition.sources entry of an admitted `@workspace` that names no root, and a claimant selection the dialect does not read (absolute, ./ or with an empty or .. segment), which selects no path; they are not admission findings. @inventarch/distribution's workspace session adds validation(), admission's findings with an IA-COMP-FIELD-VALUE warning at the line of each such declaration, in the report's order, and validateWorkspace returns it; ia validate, ia capture and ia-distribution validate all report that one finding set, and ia doctor counts its warnings, so such a typo is seen where it is authored. The session's admission() and the ia compile artifact are unchanged. The @inventarch/cli SPEC states the dialect; no ia command resolves a path yet, and the frozen Door routes and ia scope are unchanged. Workspace revisions and per-record digests are unchanged; the .ia/.iadb/graph.json cache payload gains the two indexes, so an existing cache file is rewritten on the next open.

### Every `ia` consumer command refusal names one next command

Every consumer command refusal now carries a next action: the --json refusal object always has a non-empty string next, where a refusal raised by a service used to carry null, and human output prints the same text on the → line without breaking the command it quotes across lines. The text quotes exactly one command and spells no other command or option beside it; remedies that used to list alternative flags or sources now name one command, and a service message may still name the alternatives. The command is an ia command, except that a recovery or a directory registry repair names ia-distribution, the binary of @inventarch/distribution, which a global install of @inventarch/cli does not put on PATH. It carries the values the invocation supplied that it needs, such as the root (the target directory for ia init), host, package ids, registry or catalog source, descriptor or --force. Except after an unknown or interrupted command, which repeat the invocation as typed (below), a command that reruns the refused one repeats no output flag such as --json, and a rerun of an install or update names its plan, without --apply; a rerun of ia restore carries --apply --yes, the rerun after deleting a corrupt cached archive drops --offline so the archive is fetched again, and a placeholder such as `<directory>`, `<path>`, `<file>` or `<url|dir>` stands only for the value to correct or supply. It may begin with one manual step, such as deleting a named file, replacing a link under .ia/src, mapping a provider in .ia/registries.json or setting IA_HOME. A workspace whose sources cannot be read, such as one with a link under .ia/src, names that repair and then ia validate for its root. A service refusal without a remedy of its own names the command's --help for a usage error, ia validate for a record finding (IA-COMP, IA-GRAPH and IA-LANG codes) and ia doctor for any other code, with the root the invocation gave. An unknown command names the nearest one with the rest of the invocation as typed, keeping only --root and --params for a machine operation. A file the invocation named that is not there (the --descriptor of ia pack, the --catalog or --requests of ia install, update and restore) names the same command with that one value as `<file>`. ia host with a --root that does not exist now refuses as IA-DB-ROOT-INVALID, as every other command does, instead of IA-CLI-FAILED: it used to fail inside its own error handling, so the exit class stays 3 and only the defect's code changes. An interrupted command (exit 130) gains next in its JSON object and on its stderr line: the rerun that finishes work the command had already written, or otherwise the same invocation as typed, with any --apply, --yes and --json it was given. Many next texts are reworded, so automation should match the code, not the sentence. ia compile, deprecated in favour of ia capture and otherwise unchanged in 1.x (its alias of ia capture lands in 2.0), keeps every refusal's code, exit class and location: an existing artifact without --force names the same invocation with --force, an --out outside .ia/work/ names it with `--out <file>`, and --out with --stdout names ia compile --help. The nine machine protocol operations and their output are unchanged. Code that imports the published internal subpaths keeps its 1.x forms: in @inventarch/cli/internal/consumer the Refusal constructor's where and next still default to null, refusalOf(error) without the refused invocation still returns a null next, and renderRefusal gains an optional refused argument and gives a refusal without a next action the fallback above, so the --json next is a string even then; refusalOf(error, refused) spells that fallback itself. In @inventarch/cli/internal/distribute, PlanRequest gains an optional reruns field; without it collectPlan spells each remedy's command from the request's own fields, and every refusal keeps its code. The exported UNMAPPED text is unchanged. In the 1.x collectPlan request form, a missing --catalog or --requests file now throws the CLI Refusal, with the same code IA-DIST-INPUT-INVALID and exit 3 and a next, where it threw the service DistributionError; collectPlan already threw Refusal for its other refusals in 1.x, so callers should match the code.

### Retained previous snapshot; staleness and readiness by digest

@inventarch/db handles now retain the previous admitted root snapshot beside the current one: the most recent root snapshot whose revision differs from the current revision. Opening or refreshing at an unchanged revision keeps it; only a revision change rotates the current snapshot into previous, and a failed refresh rotates nothing. Handle, ReadHandle and the editor reader add previousRevision; staleness(identity, options), which answers unchanged, changed, new or removed by comparing the identity's current and previous membership digests (every current identity reads new while no previous snapshot is retained); and readiness(identity, digest, options), which answers current, previous or unknown for a digest observed earlier, such as an `@observation`'s subject-revision. Both bind within, root, phase and revision as get does, answer about the record get returns under those options, and refuse an identity outside a scope with IA-DB-OUT-OF-SCOPE. Only root snapshots are retained, so when a narrower root or phase reads an occurrence other than the root one, staleness is undefined and readiness answers only current or unknown; a read through a scope token never reports removed. The Staleness and Readiness types are exported. The pair persists between processes as one capture pair, .ia/work/snapshot/current.json and previous.json, ia-snapshot-1 documents that only a capture writes: writeCapture(root, text), exported with CAPTURE_FORMAT, CAPTURE_DIRECTORY, CAPTURE_CURRENT, CAPTURE_PREVIOUS and the CaptureWrite type, writes the document as current.json, counts its records against the prior capture by digest, renames the prior current.json to previous.json on a revision change, refuses a link, an entry at .ia/work or .ia/work/snapshot that is not a directory, or a non-regular entry at either snapshot path with IA-DB-PATH-UNSAFE before writing anything, and leaves the pair it found when a write fails. Every disk handle, with the cache enabled or not, seeds its previous snapshot from that pair the first time it is asked, never on open or refresh, and never writes it: at the captured revision previous is the capture at previous.json, at another revision the capture at current.json. capturedRevision names the revision of the capture at current.json. A file that is no capture, a link or a non-regular entry is ignored, and a current.json that names the fresh revision with other rows seeds nothing; a capture at another revision cannot be verified and is trusted as written. A refresh rotates the pair in memory without reading the capture pair and reads capturedRevision again when next asked, so a handle that lived through an edit reports it whether or not it was captured, and one that refreshes often holds one pair, and the cache-free editor reader retains nothing. No file under .ia/.iadb holds retention, so the graph cache is unchanged. Workspace revisions, snapshots, the frozen Door operations and ia scope are unchanged, and no frozen route reads the capture pair.
